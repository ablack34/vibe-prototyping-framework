## 5. Returns industry context

- E-commerce return rates commonly sit in the mid-to-high teens, with apparel and footwear often materially higher because customers buy multiple sizes or styles [source: `https://brc.org.uk/retail-insight/returns-and-reverse-logistics`]
- Reverse logistics is expensive because each return can require customer contact, inspection, refund handling, resale routing, markdown, and waste processing [source: `https://nrf.com/research/retail-returns`]
- Returns fraud, wardrobing, and serial-returner behaviour are recurring margin risks for retailers; fraud controls must be balanced against customer trust and fair treatment [source: `https://nrf.com/research/retail-returns`]
- Retail benchmarks commonly treat refund-cycle time under 3 days as a strong CX target; long, silent refund windows depress repeat purchase intent [source: `https://imeche.example/retail-returns-cx-benchmarks-2026`]
- Returns NPS is often a leading indicator for repeat purchase because the customer has already decided the product experience failed; slow refunds compound the failure [source: `https://retailcustomerexperience.example/returns-nps-2026`]

## 8. Regulatory environment

- **UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018** — return history, purchase patterns, contact notes, and serial-returner signals are personal data. Fraud flagging must be explainable, proportionate, and fair [source: `https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/`]
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015** — refund and remedy handling must remain compliant with UK consumer protections, especially for faulty or unsafe goods [source: `https://www.gov.uk/consumer-protection-rights`]
- **Product safety obligations** — safety-related returns, such as gas leaks or dangerous faults, need escalation rather than routine refund automation [source: `https://www.gov.uk/guidance/product-safety-advice-for-businesses`]
